# Search and Selection

When performing a search or selection, **every step must be recorded** to ensure transparency and reproducibility. The final result of this process is documented in the PRISMA flowchart.

## 1. The Selection Process Flow
A typical selection process follows these sequential steps:
1. **Merge Results:** Combine search results from all queried databases.
2. **Duplicate Removal:** Remove duplicate records (automated + manual).
3. **Title-Abstract Selection:** Review titles and abstracts against pre-established eligibility criteria to remove obviously irrelevant reports.
4. **Full-Text Retrieval:** Obtain full texts for the remaining articles. Link multiple reports of the same study together.
5. **Full-Text Selection:** Read the full texts and make final inclusion/exclusion decisions based on advanced criteria.
6. **Correspondence:** Contact authors for clarification if needed.
7. **Final Decision & Notes:** Finalize the included studies and make notes on any ongoing trials.

## 2. Duplicate Removal
- **Goal:** Including duplicates leads to data distortion and bias. It must be minimized.
- **Hints for manual checking:** 
  - Compare trial identification numbers.
  - Check author names, location, and study settings.
  - Compare intervention details and participant baseline data.
  - Check trial date/duration.
- **Resolution:** If uncertain whether a study is a duplicate, contact the corresponding author.

## 3. Independent Selection and Disagreements
- **Two Independent Reviewers:** The selection (both title-abstract and full-text phases) MUST be done by at least 2 people independently based on previously defined criteria.
- **Hierarchy of Criteria:** List inclusion/exclusion criteria based on importance. If a study fails the most important criterion, exclude it immediately.
- **Documenting Exclusions:** The reason for exclusion at the full-text stage must be documented (e.g., wrong population, wrong intervention). 
- **Resolving Disagreements:** Disagreements must be discussed between the two reviewers. If unresolved, a third independent person is involved to break the tie.

## 4. Cohen’s Kappa Score
- A statistical measure of inter-rater agreement between the two independent reviewers.
- Calculates how much an author’s selections matched the other author's selections during the title-abstract and full-text phases.

## Agent Directives
- **MANDATORY:** Emphasize that screening MUST be performed by two independent reviewers to minimize bias.
- **MANDATORY:** Warn the user that exact search strings, database names, and search dates must be recorded to accurately construct the PRISMA flowchart later.
- **MANDATORY:** Remind users to prioritize and clearly document reasons for exclusion during the full-text screening phase.
