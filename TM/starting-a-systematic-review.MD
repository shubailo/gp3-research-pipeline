# Starting a Systematic Review

## 1. Why Do a Systematic Review?
Systematic reviews are performed because everyone NEEDS reliable, accessible scientific knowledge. They serve to:
- **Stay Up-to-Date:** Synthesize the massive and rapidly growing volume of scientific literature.
- **Provide the Best Evidence:** Move past individual, possibly outdated opinions to evidence-based approaches.
- **Inform Decision-Making:** Create a clear, reliable, and concise summary for patients, practitioners, policymakers, and caregivers.
- **Identify Future Needs:** Shed light on what needs to be researched in the future.

## 2. Who Should Do a Systematic Review?
Systematic reviews MUST be a team effort. A robust review team should include:
- **Clinical Experts:** Specialists in the field/subject under review (preferably from diverse sub-fields).
- **Methodologists & Statisticians:** To ensure rigor, less bias, and accurate data handling.
- **Stakeholders (Recommended):** Caregivers, patient groups, policymakers, and industry partners to ensure the review remains relevant to the ultimate beneficiaries (the patients).

## 3. The Protocol (A Priori Planning)
Conducting a systematic review is a complex, multi-step process. To reduce bias and ensure transparency, a protocol MUST be developed and published *before* starting the review.
- **Question First, Evidence Second:** The review question must be at the forefront. Gathering evidence before designing the protocol leads to bias.
- **Question Drives Methodology:** The exact methods must be chosen based on what is needed to answer the question, not the other way around.
- **Publishing the Protocol:** Reduces the risk of duplicate studies and forces the review team to stick to the original design (preventing data-driven adjustments).

## 4. Data Management and Quality Assurance
Even with experts and a protocol, a systematic review requires meticulous data management.
- **Reproducibility:** All decisions made, steps taken, data collected, and adjustments to the protocol MUST be thoroughly recorded.
- **Auditable:** The entire process must be traceable by an auditor (another reviewer or supervisor).
- **Transparency:** The review must be transparent about funding, methodology, and execution.

## Agent Directives
- **MANDATORY:** Check if a multi-disciplinary team is planned or exists for the proposed review.
- **MANDATORY:** Emphasize the necessity of registering a protocol (e.g., PROSPERO) before any data extraction begins.
- **MANDATORY:** Ensure the review question strictly drives the methodology. Do not adjust the research question to fit "better" results during the process.
