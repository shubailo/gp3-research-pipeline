# Types of Systematic Reviews (by Clinical Question)

Systematic reviews can be classified based on the clinical question they aim to answer. Different question types require specific formulation frameworks.

## 1. Interventional Reviews
- **Objective:** Compare two or more different interventions.
- **Framework:** **PICO** (Patients, Intervention, Comparator, Outcome)
  - *Example:* "Should we administer beta blockers (I) vs placebo (C) in acute myocardial infarction patients (P) to reduce 28-day mortality (O)?"

## 2. Prevalence or Incidence Reviews
- **Objective:** Measure disease burden. Prevalence is the proportion of a population with a disease; incidence is how often it occurs.
- **Framework:** **CoCoPop** (Condition, Context, Population)
  - *Example:* "What is the prevalence of claustrophobia (Condition) during any MRI scan in developed countries (Context) among adult patients (Population)?"

## 3. Diagnostic Test Accuracy Reviews
- **Objective:** Summarize test performance to help clinicians determine accuracy.
- **Framework:** **PIRD** (Patients, Index Test, Reference Standard, Diagnosis)
  - *Example:* "What is the accuracy of wrist MRI (I) compared to Arthroscopy (R) for cartilaginous lesions (D) in patients with suspected lesions (P)?"

## 4. Etiology / Risk Factor Reviews
- **Objective:** Determine if a relationship exists between an exposure and a health outcome.
- **Framework:** **PEO** (Population, Exposure, Outcome)
  - *Example:* "Are adults (P) exposed to radon (E) at risk for developing lung cancer (O)?"

## 5. Prognostic Reviews
- **Objective:** Identify factors associated with a particular disease or outcome.
- **Framework:** Population, Prognostic factor (independent variable), Outcome (dependent variable).
  - *Example:* "In adults with low back pain (P), what is the association between recovery expectations (Prognostic Factor) and disability outcomes (O)?"

## The Preliminary Search
A preliminary search is required before formally starting the systematic review.
- **Purpose:** Make sure the topic is feasible, doable, and not already done.
- **Limitations:** It is NOT exhaustive and should not be used as the sole source of data.
- **Required Steps:**
  1. **Check PROSPERO:** Ensure no one else is currently working on the same idea to avoid duplication.
  2. **Test Search:** Use unfinalized keywords on main databases.
  3. **Locate Key Articles:** You MUST find at least **3-5 key articles** that meet your criteria. If you find them, the question is feasible.

## Hypothesis Formation
- A feasible research question must have a specific aim and hypothesis. 
- You must know *why* you are asking the question and *what* implications it has for practice and research.

## Agent Directives
- **MANDATORY:** Select the correct framework (PICO, CoCoPop, PIRD, PEO) based on the type of systematic review the user wants to conduct.
- **MANDATORY:** Ensure the user has conducted a preliminary search yielding at least 3-5 key articles before proceeding to protocol design.
- **MANDATORY:** Verify the idea against PROSPERO to avoid duplication.
