# Protocol Registration

Prospective submission and publication of key information about the design of a systematic review or meta-analysis is **inevitable and mandatory**.

## 1. Why Register a Protocol?
Protocol registration helps ensure studies are conducted to the highest standards, leading to reproducible evidence. It facilitates:
- Careful planning
- Avoidance of duplication (redundant analyses)
- Reduction of reporting bias

## 2. Avoiding Reporting Biases
Registering a protocol prospectively mitigates several forms of bias:
- **Publication Bias:** Studies with positive results are more likely to be accepted than those with negative results.
- **Time-Lag Bias:** Positive findings are published faster than negative ones.
- **Location Bias:** Positive/significant results lead to multiple publications, altering accessibility.
- **Citation Bias:** Citing only studies that support one's results (a red flag for peer reviewers).
- **Language Bias:** Positive results are more likely to be published in English-language journals.
- **Selective Reporting Bias:** Measuring multiple outcomes but only reporting the positive ones. *Rule: You must report ALL outcomes planned in the protocol, irrespective of whether they are positive or negative.*

## 3. PROSPERO Database
**PROSPERO** is the largest, peer-reviewed, open-access international database of prospectively registered systematic reviews.
- PRISMA-P recommended.
- Free-of-charge.
- Provides a citable registration number upon acceptance.
- Highly recommended: Search PROSPERO *first* to ensure the same review is not already ongoing. You can also read published protocols (e.g., Cochrane Collaboration) to learn best practices.

## 4. Registration Guidelines and Timeline
- **Timing:** You MUST register the protocol *before data extraction begins*.
- **Language:** Written in English (future tense).
- **Key Elements:** Requires a clear PICO question and detailed methodological description.
- **Updates:** You can update the review protocol at any time with a short explanation, though the peer-review process takes weeks.
- **Common Mistakes:** Delayed or no protocol registration; poorly designed protocols.

## Agent Directives
- **MANDATORY:** Before executing any data extraction, prompt the user to confirm that their protocol is registered (preferably on PROSPERO).
- **MANDATORY:** Ensure the protocol is drafted in the **future tense** and includes a strict **PICO** definition.
- **MANDATORY:** If the user attempts to drop an outcome that was planned in the protocol because it is "negative" or "insignificant," warn them against **Selective Reporting Bias**.
