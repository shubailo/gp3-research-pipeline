# Determining the Scope of the Review

Every systematic review starts with a focused, well-framed question. The methodology must follow the question, not the other way around. The primary consideration is whether the review serves decision-makers, and ultimately, the patients.

## 1. The FINER Framework (Validation)
Before defining the scope, validate the core review question using the FINER framework:
- **F**easible: Are there eligible primary studies? (Avoid "empty reviews" unless clinically justified).
- **I**nteresting: Relevant beyond academia (interests patients and healthcare partners).
- **N**ovel: Avoid duplicating existing reviews. (Check PROSPERO registry).
- **E**thical: Aligns with medical ethics, avoids conflicts of interest.
- **R**elevant: Puts patients first and addresses actual healthcare needs.

## 2. PICO Frameworks
The PICO model (Patients, Interventions, Control, Outcome) clearly communicates context. When defining the scope, consider three levels of PICO frames:
1. **The Review PICO:** The overarching context of the study ("what is my study about").
2. **Synthesis PICOs:** The specific frames for each individual analysis within the study.
3. **Included Study PICOs:** The specific frames of each primary study included in the analysis.

## 3. Review Scope: Broad vs. Narrow
The review question can be broad or narrow; both have trade-offs.
- **Narrow Scope:** Highly specific PICO (e.g., "Does aspirin reduce the risk of stroke in elderly patients with breast cancer?"). Easier to handle and apply in specific practice.
- **Broad Scope:** Comprehensive PICO (e.g., "Do anti-platelet agents reduce thrombotic events in humans?"). Higher generalizability, summarizes a wider array of knowledge, but introduces more heterogeneity.

## 4. Handling Heterogeneity: Lumping vs. Splitting
When combining diverse studies (comparing "apples and oranges"), authors must decide how to handle heterogeneity. This exists on a continuum:
- **Lumping:** Combining studies (e.g., grouping all newer generation antidepressants together).
- **Splitting:** Taking studies separately (e.g., analyzing each specific antidepressant individually).
- **Alternatives:** Subgroup analyses (separating by patient age or drug type) or Network Meta-Analysis (simultaneous comparison of multiple PICO elements).

## 5. Priority-Setting and Stakeholders
- **Priority-setting exercises:** Clarify which uncertainties need resolving by engaging stakeholders (policymakers, practitioners, guideline developers).
- **Benefits of engagement:** Increases relevance, promotes mutual learning, improves research uptake, and reduces research waste.
- **Health Equity (PROGRESS-Plus):** Ensure the scope does not involuntarily exclude populations. Consider Place of residence, Race/ethnicity, Occupation, Gender/sex, Religion, Education, Socio-economic status, Social capital, Plus (age, disability, sexual orientation).

## 6. Conceptual Frameworks and Logic Models
To avoid conceptual incoherence, define key concepts and document decisions.
- **Tools:** Taxonomies, generic frameworks (TIDier, iCAT-SR), and core outcome sets.
- **Logic Models:** Graphical representations of the conceptualization phase (networking patients, interventions, and outcomes) to keep the review on topic.

## 7. Changing the Review Question and Contingencies
- **Refining the Question:** If challenges arise (e.g., eligibility criteria need modifying), record and report the reasons meticulously to avoid introducing bias.
- **Sparse Data:** Build contingencies for sparse data by considering a broader scope to include a larger volume of evidence.
- **Economic Data:** Consider including cost, resource use, affordability, and cost-effectiveness in the conceptual framework if relevant to decision-makers.

## Agent Directives
- **MANDATORY:** Always frame the research question using the **PICO** format.
- **MANDATORY:** explicitly state whether the proposed scope is **Broad** or **Narrow**, and justify the choice regarding heterogeneity (Lumping vs. Splitting).
- **RECOMMENDED:** Consider Health Equity (PROGRESS-Plus) when defining the Patient (P) criteria.
