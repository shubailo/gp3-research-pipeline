# PROSPERO Protokoll Tervezet: TRPA1 Agonisták Humán Modelljei

Ez a dokumentum a "Topikális TRPA1 agonisták okozta lokális neurogén gyulladás, hőmérséklet-változás és fájdalom egészséges embereken" téma alapján készült PROSPERO protokoll tervezet. A keresési stratégiát a megadott `Systematic-search.MD` irányelvei alapján alakítottam ki.

## Review title
Local temperature changes and hyperalgesia induced by topical TRPA1 agonists in healthy humans: a systematic review and meta-analysis of experimental models

## Condition or domain being studied
Neurogenic inflammation; Pain; Hyperalgesia; Thermoregulation (Local Skin Temperature); TRPA1 channel activation

## Rationale for the review
Experimental human models of pain and inflammation are crucial for understanding neurogenic mechanisms and testing novel analgesics. Topical application of Transient Receptor Potential Ankyrin 1 (TRPA1) agonists, such as cinnamaldehyde and allyl isothiocyanate (mustard oil), reliably induces local flare (vasodilation/temperature increase) and secondary hyperalgesia in healthy volunteers. While widely used, the magnitude, reliability, and dose-response characteristics of these thermal and algesic responses have not been quantitatively synthesized. A meta-analysis of these models is needed to standardize experimental protocols for future phase 1 analgesic trials.

## Review objectives
1. **Primary objective:** To quantify the magnitude of local skin temperature changes and blood flow (flare) induced by topical or intradermal TRPA1 agonists compared to placebo in healthy human volunteers.
2. **Secondary objective:** To evaluate the extent of hyperalgesia and allodynia (measured by Quantitative Sensory Testing - QST) induced by TRPA1 activation, and compare the effects of different agonists (e.g., cinnamaldehyde vs. mustard oil).

## PICO (Eligibility criteria)

### Population (P)
**Included:** Healthy adult human volunteer subjects (aged ≥ 18 years).
**Excluded:** Patients with pre-existing acute or chronic pain conditions, neuropathy, or inflammatory skin conditions.

### Intervention (I)
**Included:** Administration of specific TRPA1 agonists (e.g., cinnamaldehyde, allyl isothiocyanate/mustard oil). Application methods can include topical (cream/patch) or intradermal injection.

### Comparator (C)
**Included:** Placebo or vehicle control administration (e.g., ethanol, mineral oil, or inactive cream).

### Outcomes (O)
**Primary Outcomes:**
- Local skin temperature increase (measured in °C using thermography or thermistors).
- Local blood flow/perfusion changes (flare area or flux measured via Laser Doppler Flowmetry/Imaging).

**Secondary Outcomes:**
- Area of secondary hyperalgesia (cm²).
- Pain intensity ratings (VAS/NRS).
- Changes in mechanical or thermal pain thresholds (QST).

### Study design
**Included:** Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs), including crossover and parallel-group experimental studies in healthy volunteers.
**Excluded:** Observational studies, animal studies, in vitro experiments, and non-randomized trials. Gray literature (e.g., conference abstracts) will be excluded to maintain high methodological quality, unless pre-specified.

## Search strategy
In accordance with standard meta-analysis guidelines, we will search the following mandatory primary databases:
- **MEDLINE (PubMed)**
- **Embase**
- **Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL)**

Since this involves experimental interventions in humans, we will also search:
- **ClinicalTrials.gov**
- **WHO ICTRP**

## Data extraction (selection and coding)
Two independent reviewers will screen titles and abstracts, followed by full-text screening. Disagreements will be resolved by a third investigator. Data extraction will include study characteristics, participant demographics, agonist type and dose, application method, measurement modalities (e.g., Laser Doppler, Thermography, QST), and quantitative outcome data (mean, SD/SEM, n).

## Risk of bias (quality) assessment
Two independent reviewers will assess the risk of bias using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) tool for randomized crossover and parallel trials.

## Strategy for data synthesis
Continuous outcomes will be pooled using Standardized Mean Differences (SMD, specifically Hedges' g to correct for small sample sizes typical in volunteer studies) with 95% Confidence Intervals (CI), as measurement units (e.g., Perfusion Units vs. °C) vary across studies. Random-effects models will be employed due to expected methodological heterogeneity (e.g., varying agonist concentrations).
