## 12. Rationale
Obesity has reached pandemic proportions, affecting over 1 billion individuals globally and contributing directly to cardiovascular morbidity, type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), and premature mortality. Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs; e.g., semaglutide, liraglutide) and dual incretin mimetics (e.g., tirzepatide) have revolutionized obesity treatment by inducing substantial weight loss. However, their comprehensive biological effects on human thermoregulation, basal metabolic rate, and heat dissipation remain a major unresolved dilemma:

1. **Energy Expenditure and Metabolic Adaptation:** Traditional caloric restriction triggers adaptive thermogenesis, wherein resting energy expenditure (REE) falls disproportionately below that predicted by loss of fat-free mass (FFM), contributing to weight loss plateaus and recidivism. Preclinical and early clinical investigations report conflicting findings regarding whether GLP-1 RAs preserve or stimulate REE and fat oxidation (lower RQ/RER) through central sympathetic activation and brown adipose tissue (BAT) recruitment, or whether their actions are solely driven by hypophagia.
2. **Heat Balance and Summer Heat Vulnerability:** Thermoregulation dictates that core body temperature ($T_c$) reflects the balance between heat production, environmental heat load, and heat dissipation. Obese individuals inherently exhibit impaired heat dissipation due to lower body surface area-to-mass ratios and the insulation provided by subcutaneous adipose tissue. GLP-1 RAs are known to induce central hypodipsia (suppression of thirst sensation) and frequent gastrointestinal adverse effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), which together promote hypovolemia. Dehydration suppresses cutaneous vasodilation and eccrine sweating—the primary avenues of heat dissipation in humans.
3. **Clinical Urgency:** If GLP-1 RAs either stimulate endogenous thermogenesis or, more critically, impair heat dissipation via hypodipsia/dehydration, patients exposed to high ambient temperatures ($T_a > 30\text{--}35\,^{\circ}\text{C}$) face heightened susceptibility to heat exhaustion and life-threatening heat stroke. Clarifying these mechanisms across clinical trials is critical to inform international clinical guidelines and patient safety protocols.

## 13. Objectives
- **Primary Objective:** To systematically evaluate the impact of GLP-1 receptor agonists and dual incretin mimetics on core body temperature, resting energy expenditure (REE/RMR), substrate oxidation (RQ/RER, fat oxidation), and heat-related adverse events in adults with overweight or obesity.
- **Secondary Objectives:**
  1. To determine whether energy expenditure changes persist after rigorous adjustment for fat-free mass (FFM).
  2. To differentiate acute pharmacological effects (single-dose or $\le 7$ days, before substantial weight loss occurs) from chronic adaptation ($\ge 4$ weeks, following weight loss).
  3. To compare pure GLP-1 RAs versus dual/triple incretin agonists (e.g., GLP-1/GIP, GLP-1/glucagon).
  4. To evaluate the incidence of thermoregulatory and hydration-related adverse events (dehydration, hyperthermia, dizziness, syncope).

## 14. Eligibility criteria

### Population (P)
- **Included:** Adults ($\ge 18$ years of age) with overweight or obesity (BMI $\ge 25\,\text{kg/m}^2$ or $\ge 30\,\text{kg/m}^2$), with or without comorbid type 2 diabetes mellitus or prediabetes.
- **Excluded:** Pediatric patients ($<18$ years), pregnant or lactating individuals, animal studies (used exclusively for mechanistic context), and patients with active malignant hyperthermia, untreated thyroid storm, or pheochromocytoma.

### Intervention / Exposure (I)
- Monotherapy or combination therapy with approved or investigational GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., semaglutide, liraglutide, exenatide, dulaglutide, lixisenatide) or multi-incretin dual/triple agonists (e.g., tirzepatide, survodutide, retatrutide).

### Comparator (C)
- Placebo, standard lifestyle intervention / caloric restriction without incretin pharmacotherapy, active non-incretin comparator, or within-subject baseline (pre-treatment values in controlled crossover trials).

### Outcomes (O)
- **Primary Outcomes:**
  1. Core body temperature ($T_c$, measured via ingestible telemetry pill, rectal, or esophageal probe in °C) and skin temperature ($T_{sk}$).
  2. Resting energy expenditure (REE / RMR) measured by indirect calorimetry (reported in kcal/24h and/or normalized to fat-free mass as kcal/kg FFM/day).
  3. Respiratory Exchange Ratio (RER) or Respiratory Quotient (RQ).
  4. Whole-body fat/lipid oxidation rate (g/24h or mg/kg/min).
- **Secondary Outcomes:**
  1. Diet-induced thermogenesis (DIT) or postprandial thermic effect of food (TEF).
  2. Brown adipose tissue (BAT) volume and glucose uptake assessed by $^{18}\text{F}$-FDG PET/CT or infrared thermography.
  3. Autonomic heat dissipation parameters: cutaneous blood flow/vasodilation (laser Doppler), local or whole-body sweat rate.
  4. Heat- and hydration-related adverse events: reported incidence of hyperthermia, heat exhaustion, heat stroke, severe dehydration, and orthostatic syncope.

### Study types
- Randomized controlled trials (RCTs; parallel-group or crossover design).
- Non-randomized prospective controlled clinical trials and controlled physiological challenge/calorimetry chamber studies.
- Excluded: Retrospective case reports, case series ($n < 5$), narrative reviews, editorials, and conference abstracts lacking extractable quantitative data.

## 17. Main outcome(s)
1. **Change in Core Body Temperature ($T_c$):** Mean difference (MD in °C) from baseline to follow-up between GLP-1 RA and control.
2. **Change in Resting Energy Expenditure (REE):** Mean difference (MD in kcal/day) and standardized FFM-adjusted REE (kcal/kg FFM/day).
3. **Change in Fasting and Postprandial RQ/RER:** Mean difference in respiratory exchange ratio.
4. **Change in Fat Oxidation:** Mean difference (g/day or % of total energy expenditure).

## 18. Additional outcomes
Diet-induced thermogenesis (DIT, % of meal energy), brown adipose tissue SUVmax, cutaneous vascular conductance, incidence of heat-related adverse events (Risk Ratio [RR] or Odds Ratio [OR]).

## 19. Data extraction (selection and coding)
Two independent reviewers (Fanni Kovács and Teodóra Eklics) will screen titles and abstracts, followed by full-text evaluation against eligibility criteria using a standardized, piloted data extraction template. Discrepancies will be resolved by consensus or adjudication by senior supervisors (Dr. András Garami, Dr. András Nagy). Extracted items: study metadata, patient demographics (age, sex, BMI, baseline body composition), drug regimen (molecule, dose, route, duration), indirect calorimetry methodology (hood, chamber, fasting duration, acclimatization, ambient room temperature $T_a$), core temperature measurement site, and quantitative outcome data (means, SDs, SEs, 95% CIs, sample sizes).

## 20. Risk of bias (quality) assessment
- For randomized trials: **Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2)** tool across 5 domains (randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome, selection of reported results).
- For non-randomized studies: **ROBINS-I** tool.
Assessments will be conducted independently by two reviewers with visual traffic-light plots generated.

## 21. Strategy for data synthesis
- Random-effects meta-analysis models (DerSimonian-Laird and Restricted Maximum Likelihood [REML]) will be synthesized to account for expected between-study clinical diversity.
- Continuous outcomes will be pooled as weighted Mean Differences (MD) or Standardized Mean Differences (SMD) with 95% confidence intervals.
- Dichotomous adverse event outcomes will be pooled as Risk Ratios (RR) or Odds Ratios (OR) with 95% CIs.
- Statistical heterogeneity will be quantified using $I^2$ and $\tau^2$.
- Publication bias will be evaluated via funnel plots and Egger's linear regression test if $\ge 10$ studies are available.
- Certainty of evidence will be appraised via the **GRADE** (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation) framework, generating a Summary of Findings (SoF) table.

## 22. Analysis of subgroups or subsets
Pre-specified subgroup and meta-regression analyses:
1. **Pharmacological Class:** Pure GLP-1 RAs (semaglutide, liraglutide, exenatide) vs. Dual/Triple agonists (tirzepatide, survodutide, retatrutide).
2. **Exposure Duration:** Acute phase (single dose to $\le 7$ days prior to significant weight change) vs. Chronic phase ($\ge 4$ weeks, with established weight reduction).
3. **Calorimetric Normalization:** Absolute REE (kcal/day) vs. Fat-Free Mass-adjusted REE (kcal/kg FFM/day).
4. **Comorbidity Status:** Individuals with T2DM vs. non-diabetic obesity.
5. **Ambient Temperature and Seasonality:** Studies controlling ambient room temperature ($T_a = 22\text{--}24\,^{\circ}\text{C}$ thermoneutral) vs. non-standardized settings.
