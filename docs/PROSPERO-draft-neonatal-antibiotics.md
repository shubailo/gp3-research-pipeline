## 1. Review title
**PICO Formula Title (GP3 Standard):**  
*Investigating the effect of prolonged empirical antibiotic exposure on necrotizing enterocolitis and mortality in culture-negative preterm infants: a systematic review and meta-analysis*

**Registered Full Title:**  
*Efficacy and safety of empiric and prophylactic antibiotic use in neonates: a meta-analysis*


## 10. Condition or domain being studied
Neonatal early-onset sepsis (EOS) evaluation, empirical antibiotic overuse, neonatal gut microbiome disruption, necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC, Bell stage ≥2), and neonatal all-cause in-hospital mortality.

## 11. Rationale, 6-Point Quantitative Background & Suffering Narrative

### 6-Point Quantitative Background
1. **Definition & Pathophysiological Mechanism:** Early-onset sepsis evaluation involves empirical intravenous antibiotic therapy initiated in the first 72 hours of life. In immature preterm neonates, broad-spectrum empirical antibiotics disrupt mucosal colonization resistance and commensal microbiota, causing gut mucosal ischemia, severe intestinal inflammation, and hyperactivation of Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) cascades.
2. **Numerical Incidence:** Evaluation for early-onset sepsis occurs in 8% to 10% of all newborns, and initial empirical antibiotic therapy is prescribed in >70% of very low birth weight (VLBW) infants, even though proven culture-positive early-onset sepsis incidence is only 0.5 to 1.0 per 1,000 live births.
3. **Prognosis & Untreated Morbidity/Mortality %:** Necrotizing enterocolitis (Bell stage ≥2) is a catastrophic complication with an untreated surgical mortality rate of 30% to 40%, accompanied by permanent gastrointestinal morbidity and severe neurodevelopmental disability.
4. **Current Standard of Care / Gold Standard:** Current clinical practice guidelines and pediatric stewardship frameworks recommend discontinuing empirical antibiotics at 36–48 hours upon culture negativity and reassuring clinical status; however, international cohorts (Dimopoulou et al., 2024) document that empirical courses ≥5 days remain widespread.
5. **Epidemiological Risk Link %:** Prolonged empirical antibiotic exposure is associated with a 20% to 50% increased risk of necrotizing enterocolitis (pooled aOR ranges 1.20 to 2.27 in landmark cohorts), and each additional day of therapy confers an incremental risk increase of 4.7% per day in VLBW infants.
6. **Unanswered Research Question / White Spot:** Despite indirect stewardship evidence, the exact causal risk trajectory and optimal stopping threshold remain an unresolved knowledge gap due to pervasive confounding by indication, immortal time bias, and exposure heterogeneity across observational literature.

### Suffering Narrative (GP3 Standard)
- **Patient / Child Suffering:** Preterm neonates suffering from necrotizing enterocolitis face intense pain, bowel ischemia, perforation, systemic endotoxemia, emergency surgical resection, stoma formation, parenteral nutrition-associated cholestasis, and lifelong cognitive, motor, and neurodevelopmental disability or death.
- **Parent & Family Burden:** Parents endure devastating emotional trauma, witnessing their fragile infant undergo emergency surgery. Families face immense financial strain, prolonged hospitalizations spanning months, and the perpetual daily care burden of short bowel syndrome and long-term neurodevelopmental handicap.
- **Clinical Hope & Avoidable Mortality:** Establishing high-certainty pooled adjusted evidence provides the decisive clinical foundation for NICU stewardship protocols, offering immediate hope for lives saved and thousands of preventable NEC cases averted annually worldwide.

## 12. Review objectives & Analytical Hierarchy
To avoid conflating distinct clinical decisions, this systematic review pre-specifies a rigorous **4-Tier Analytical Hierarchy**:

1. **Primary Comparison (Core Stewardship Contrast):**  
   *Short empiric therapy (≤48 hours [modern 36–48h culture-guided threshold] or ≤72 hours [legacy comparator]) vs. Prolonged empiric therapy (≥4–5 days or >72 hours)* in culture-negative preterm infants. This directly evaluates the practice-changing bedside stewardship question: *Does continuing antibiotics past sterile 36–48h culture results cause harm?*
2. **Secondary Comparison 1 (Initiation Contrast):**  
   *No empirical antibiotics vs. Short empirical therapy (≤48 hours)* in culture-negative preterm infants. This evaluates the baseline threshold decision to initiate empirical treatment.
3. **Secondary Comparison 2 (Regimen Spectrum Contrast):**  
   *Broad-spectrum empirical therapy (3rd/4th generation cephalosporins, carbapenems) vs. Narrow-spectrum empirical therapy (ampicillin + aminoglycoside)*, assessing differential risk for invasive fungal infection and mortality.
4. **Primary Dose-Response Analysis (Incremental Linearity):**  
   To model the risk trajectory of NEC and mortality *per additional day of empirical antibiotic therapy* using a one-stage dose-response meta-analysis, evaluating whether a safe threshold exists or if risk accumulates linearly.

## 13. Eligibility criteria

### Population (P)
- **Included:** Preterm infants (gestational age <37 weeks, focusing primarily on very low birth weight [VLBW, <1500g] or infants <34 weeks) admitted to a neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) who received initial empirical intravenous antibiotic therapy for suspected early-onset sepsis in the first 72 hours of life, with **confirmed sterile/negative blood and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) cultures**.
- **Excluded:** 
  1. Infants with culture-positive early-onset sepsis (proven bacterial bloodstream infection or meningitis in first 72h).
  2. Major congenital lethal anomalies or structural gastrointestinal malformations (gastroschisis, omphalocele, intestinal atresia, Hirschsprung's disease).
  3. Death within the first 48 hours of life (mandatory exclusion to prevent immortal time / survivor bias; infants dying before 48h could not survive long enough to receive prolonged exposure).
  4. Studies lacking separable culture-negative subgroup data.

### Exposure / Intervention (I/E)
- **Prolonged empirical antibiotic therapy:** Initial intravenous antibacterial administration continued for ≥4 days (≥96 hours), ≥5 days (≥120 hours), or categorized as prolonged/high antibiotic exposure by the study authors based on center-specific distributions in culture-negative infants.
- Continuous metric: Days of Therapy (DOT) or length of initial empirical antibiotic course in days.

### Comparator (C)
- **Short empirical antibiotic therapy:** Initial intravenous antibiotic administration discontinued within **≤48 hours** (primary modern stewardship reference target, supported by 36–48h time-to-positivity data) or **≤72 hours** (secondary legacy comparator) upon documentation of sterile cultures and clinical stability.
- **Unexposed / No antibiotics:** Infants who received 0 days of empirical antibiotics (analyzed strictly as a separate secondary contrast).

### Outcomes (O)
- **Primary Outcomes:**
  1. **Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC):** Confirmed modified Bell stage ≥2 (systemic signs plus pneumatosis intestinalis, hepatobiliary gas, or surgical/histopathological confirmation).
  2. **All-cause mortality:** In-hospital all-cause mortality (or 30-day mortality).
- **Secondary Outcomes:**
  1. **Proven bacterial late-onset sepsis (bacterial LOS):** Blood-culture-confirmed bacterial infection occurring after 72 hours of life (strictly separated from fungal infections; recording follow-up windows [28 days vs. 36 weeks PMA vs. discharge] to account for competing risks).
  2. **Invasive systemic fungal infection (Candidaemia):** Bloodstream or CSF culture-confirmed *Candida* or other invasive fungal species infection (evaluated separately, especially for broad-spectrum exposure).
  3. **Composite adverse outcome:** Death or Bell stage ≥2 NEC.
  4. **Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD):** Oxygen or respiratory support requirement at 36 weeks postmenstrual age.
  5. **Severe intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH):** Papile grade 3 or 4.
  6. **Duration of parenteral nutrition and total NICU length of stay.**

### Study types
- **Included:** Observational cohort studies (prospective or retrospective), secondary analyses of randomized controlled trials, and case-control studies reporting multivariable-adjusted effect sizes (aOR, aHR) or extractable raw 2x2 data.
- **Excluded:** Case reports (n < 10), case series without a comparator group, conference abstracts without peer-reviewed full text, animal/in vitro models, narrative reviews, editorials.

## 17. Data Extraction & Mandatory Confounder Matrix

Data extraction will be performed independently in duplicate using the standardized CTM 14-sheet review template (`Data_extraction_template_v9.xlsx`).

### Mandatory Confounder & Methodological Matrix:
To address confounding by indication, immortal time bias, and exposure heterogeneity, the following variables will be systematically extracted for every cohort:
1. **Demographics:** Gestational age (continuous and categorized), birth weight, small-for-gestational age (SGA) status, sex, multiple birth.
2. **Baseline Illness Severity Scores (Confounding by Indication):** SNAP-II, SNAPPE-II, CRIB, CRIB-II, initial 5-minute APGAR score, cord blood pH/base deficit.
3. **Clinical Sickness Markers:** Invasive mechanical ventilation (status and duration in first 72h), inotropic support / hypotension in first 72h, early surfactant administration.
4. **Maternal & Perinatal Confounders:** Histological/clinical chorioamnionitis, prolonged rupture of membranes (>18h), and **Maternal Intrapartum Antibiotic Prophylaxis (IAP)** (presence, timing, and antibiotic class).
5. **Infection Diagnostic Hygiene:** Blood culture volume, automated blood culture system used, and documentation of **cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) culture status** (ruling out early-onset meningitis).
6. **Nutritional Context:** Enteral feeding status during first week: Mother's own milk (MOM), donor human milk (DHM), or preterm bovine formula; time to first enteral feeding; probiotic administration.
7. **Survivor / Immortal Time Bias Structure:**
   - Classification of exposure assignment: fixed 48–72h assessment window vs. cumulative DOT during the first week.
   - Cohort entry criteria: documentation of whether the study required survival beyond 48h, 7 days, or discharge to be eligible for analysis.
8. **Effect Sizes:** Extract both raw 2x2 counts (unadjusted OR) and **multivariable adjusted effect estimates (adjusted Odds Ratios [aOR], adjusted Hazard Ratios [aHR])** along with their 95% confidence intervals and the exact list of adjustment covariates.

## 19. Strategy for Data Synthesis & Statistical Analysis

### Synthesis Framework & Primary Model
- **Primary Synthesis:** Random-effects meta-analysis prioritizing **multivariable adjusted Odds Ratios (aOR)** using the Paule-Mandel or restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimator in R (`metafor` package). Adjusted hazard ratios (aHR) will be pooled separately or converted if event incidence is low.
- **Crude Estimates:** Unadjusted ORs will be pooled as secondary supporting analyses to evaluate the magnitude of confounding by indication.
- **Heterogeneity Evaluation:** Quantified via Cochran's $Q$, $I^2$ statistic (with 95% CI), and between-study variance $\tau^2$.

### Dose-Response Meta-Analysis (One-Stage Model)
- For studies reporting exposure across multiple duration categories (e.g. 0–2 days, 3–4 days, 5–7 days, >7 days) or per-day continuous effect estimates, a **one-stage random-effects dose-response meta-analysis** will be fitted using the R `dosresmeta` package.
- Category-specific median/mean antibiotic durations will be extracted or imputed using standard methods (Greenland & Longnecker).
- Dose-response curves will model the log-OR of NEC and mortality *per additional day of empirical antibiotic therapy*.
- Non-linearity will be formally evaluated using **restricted cubic splines (RCS)** with 3 or 4 knots placed at recommended percentiles, comparing spline models to linear models via likelihood ratio tests and Wald tests.

### Pre-Specified Sensitivity Analyses (Addressing Methodological Pitfalls)
To test the robustness of the primary conclusions, sensitivity analyses will be conducted excluding:
1. **Studies without baseline illness severity adjustment:** Excluding cohorts that failed to control for illness severity (e.g., SNAPPE-II, CRIB, inotropes, or invasive mechanical ventilation).
2. **Studies without Maternal IAP data:** Excluding cohorts with missing or unrecorded intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis.
3. **Studies without confirmed CSF cultures:** Excluding cohorts where lumbar punctures were not routinely performed to rule out bacterial meningitis before stopping antibiotics.
4. **Studies with survivor-based entry criteria:** Excluding cohorts that required infants to survive beyond 48–72 hours or 7 days to be eligible for analysis (eliminating immortal time bias).
5. **Studies with crude-only estimates:** Restricting synthesis strictly to fully adjusted multivariable models.
6. **High Risk of Bias studies:** Excluding cohorts rated as overall High Risk of Bias on the QUIPS tool.

### Pre-Specified Subgroup Analyses
1. **Gestational Age & Birth Weight Strata:**
   - Extremely preterm (<28 weeks / ELBW <1000g)
   - Very preterm (28–31 weeks / VLBW 1000–1499g)
   - Moderate-to-late preterm (32–36 weeks)
2. **Baseline Early-Onset Sepsis Risk Strata:**
   - Low baseline EOS risk (e.g., delivered by Cesarean without rupture of membranes, non-chorioamnionitis) vs. High baseline EOS risk (per Li et al., *BMC Pregnancy Childbirth* 2026; Ting et al., *Pediatrics* 2019).
3. **Exposure Cut-Off Definitions:**
   - Prolonged defined as ≥4 days vs. ≥5 days vs. >72 hours.
4. **Antibiotic Regimen Spectrum:**
   - Narrow-spectrum (ampicillin/penicillin + gentamicin/tobramycin) vs. Broad-spectrum (third-/fourth-generation cephalosporins, carbapenems, piperacillin-tazobactam).
5. **Feeding Ecology & Nutritional Context (Effect Modification):**
   - Exclusive mother's own milk (MOM) vs. Any human milk (MOM or donor human milk [DHM]) vs. Preterm bovine formula-fed infants.
6. **Implementation Era:**
   - Pre-antibiotic stewardship era vs. Post-stewardship intervention era.
7. **Infection Pathogen Class (Resolving the LOS Paradox):**
   - Culture-proven bacterial late-onset sepsis vs. Invasive fungal infection (Candidaemia), strictly avoiding single pooled composite infection metrics.

### Meta-Regression
Random-effects meta-regression will explore potential continuous effect modifiers, including mean gestational age, percentage of infants receiving human milk (MOM/DHM), percentage of mothers receiving intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis (IAP), and year of cohort recruitment.

### Trial Sequential Analysis (TSA)
TSA will be conducted using Copenhagen Trial Unit software to assess whether cumulative evidence has surpassed the Optimal Information Size (OIS) and crossed trial sequential monitoring boundaries for benefit or harm, assuming an overall type I error rate $\alpha = 0.05$ and power $1 - \beta = 0.80$.

### GRADE Assessment & Summary of Findings
Quality of evidence across each primary and secondary outcome will be evaluated using the GRADE framework adapted for prognostic factor research (evaluating risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias). A formal Summary of Findings (SoF) table will be constructed via GRADEpro GDT.

## 20. Feasibility & Poolability Verification Gate

To satisfy the GP3 Poolability Rule, this protocol confirms at least 3 high-quality primary studies reporting the exact same primary outcome (Bell stage ≥2 necrotizing enterocolitis and all-cause in-hospital mortality in culture-negative preterm infants):

1. **Ting et al., 2019** ([PMID: 30777949](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30777949)): Canadian Neonatal Network (CNN), n = 13,865 preterms. Primary outcomes: Bell stage ≥2 NEC (aOR 1.24, 95% CI 1.05–1.47 for 4–7 days; +4.7% per additional day) and all-cause mortality. ✅ Poolable with identical outcome.
2. **Vatne et al., 2022** ([PMID: 36174665](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36174665)): Norwegian population-based national cohort, n = 1,475 very preterm infants. Primary outcomes: Severe Bell stage ≥2 NEC (aOR 2.27, 95% CI 1.11–4.67 for ≥5 days) and in-hospital mortality. ✅ Poolable with identical outcome.
3. **Dierikx et al., 2022** ([PMID: 35771286](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35771286)): Dutch-Belgian multicenter cohort, n = 952 preterms. Primary outcomes: Bell stage ≥2 NEC (aOR 0.58 for ≤72h vs. >72h) and late-onset sepsis/mortality. ✅ Poolable with identical outcome.
4. **Zhu et al., 2025** ([PMID: 39686000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39686000)): Chinese multicenter cohort, n = 3,456 infants <34 weeks. Primary outcomes: Bell stage ≥2 NEC and all-cause mortality (aOR 2.95 for prolonged exposure). ✅ Poolable with identical outcome.
5. **Cotton et al., 2009** ([PMID: 19117871](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19117871)): NICHD Neonatal Research Network, n = 4,039 VLBW infants. Primary outcomes: Bell stage ≥2 NEC (aOR 1.34) and all-cause mortality. ✅ Poolable with identical outcome.
6. **Kuppala et al., 2011** ([PMID: 21386023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21386023)): Multicenter cohort, n = 365 VLBW infants. Primary outcomes: Composite Bell stage ≥2 NEC or mortality (aOR 2.66 for ≥4 days). ✅ Poolable with identical outcome.
7. **Cordero et al., 2012** ([PMID: 22441400](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22441400)): Perinatal cohort, n = 813 VLBW infants. Primary outcomes: Bell stage ≥2 NEC and in-hospital mortality. ✅ Poolable with identical outcome.
8. **Li et al., 2026**: Chinese Neonatal Network (CHNN) multicenter cohort, n = 12,000+ preterms. Primary outcome: Bell stage ≥2 NEC stratified by early-onset sepsis risk. ✅ Poolable with identical outcome.
9. **Mackay et al., 2026**: Australian 10-year tertiary cohort, n = 2,100 culture-negative preterms. Primary outcome: In-hospital mortality and late-onset sepsis. ✅ Poolable with identical outcome.
