# How to Choose a Good Topic

The first step in conducting successful research is asking the right question. A well-chosen topic ensures scientific and practical benefit by addressing relevant knowledge gaps, improving healthcare, and maintaining researcher motivation.

## Topic Sources & Inspiration
When brainstorming new meta-analysis topics, agents and researchers should consider:
- **Scientific Literature:** Newly published articles, reviews, guidelines.
- **Scientific Community:** Topics in focus at conferences, workshops, and expert discussions.
- **Clinical Practice Gaps:** Barriers or unanswered questions identified during clinical practice.
- **Guideline Updates:** Re-evaluating existing guidelines with newly available data (updates are inevitable as science improves).
- **Expert Input:** Discussing ideas with supervisors and specialists.

## The FINER Framework
A good research question MUST satisfy the **FINER** criteria. Use this framework to validate any proposed topic:

*   **[F]easible:** It is possible to conduct the research and answer the question (e.g., sufficient primary studies exist, data is accessible).
*   **[I]nteresting:** The topic is engaging to the scientific community and has a high potential for publication in high-profile journals.
*   **[N]ovel:** The research provides new information or a significant update to previous knowledge.
*   **[E]thical:** The study conforms to ethical standards and aspects.
*   **[R]elevant:** The topic provides scientific and practical benefit, answering a question that directly helps patients, professionals, or decision-makers.

## Agent Directives
- **MANDATORY:** Before accepting a proposed topic, evaluate it strictly against the **FINER** criteria.
- **MANDATORY:** Prioritize topics that have clear clinical relevance and practical benefit over purely theoretical exercises.
