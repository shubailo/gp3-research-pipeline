# Defining the Inclusion Criteria

A distinguishing feature of a systematic review (versus a narrative review) is that authors MUST pre-specify criteria for including and excluding studies. Eligibility criteria should be based on the **PICO** elements of the review question and the types of study designs.

## 1. Defining Participants (P)
The criteria for participants should be broad enough to encompass relevant diversity, but narrow enough to provide a meaningful answer.
**Key questions to address in advance:**
- How is the disease/condition defined?
- What are the most important characteristics (demographics, age, sex, ethnicity)?
- What is the setting (hospital, community)?
- Who makes the diagnosis?
- Who should be explicitly excluded (e.g., patients with other systemic diseases)?
- **Subsets:** How will you handle studies where only a *subset* of participants is eligible? (e.g., if an age cut-off is used, what if the study mixes age groups and data cannot be separated?).

## 2. Defining Interventions and Comparators (I & C)
Specification of the interventions and comparators must be perfectly clear.
**Key questions to address in advance:**
- Is the comparator an inactive control (placebo/no intervention) or an active control (usual care/Intervention B)?
- Does the intervention have variations (dosage, intensity, mode of delivery, personnel, frequency, duration)? Are all variations included?
- Will studies using the intervention combined with another intervention (co-intervention) be included?
- How will interventions be grouped for synthesis? (e.g., grouping by dose level).

## 3. Defining Outcomes (O)
- **Warning:** Outcomes are rarely used as strict eligibility criteria for including a study.
- Systematic reviews should report all outcomes that are likely to be meaningful (avoid trivial outcomes).

## 4. Defining Eligible Study Designs
Define the eligibility criteria for study design clearly and unambiguously.
- **Rule of Thumb:** Focus on the *features* of a study's design rather than designer labels.
- **RCTs:** Always include Randomized Controlled Trials if they are feasible to conduct for your question.
- **Non-Randomized Studies:** Include these when RCTs cannot address harms/long-term outcomes, or when the intervention cannot be randomized (e.g., policy changes).

## 5. Post-Hoc Decisions and Protocol Changes
- Unanticipated issues may arise, requiring post-hoc decisions on exclusions.
- **MANDATORY:** Any changes to eligibility criteria MUST be justified. Post-hoc decisions must stay true to the review's objectives.
- **MANDATORY:** Changes to the protocol must NOT be based on the findings of the studies or the synthesis (this introduces bias).
- Document difficult decisions and test their impact using **sensitivity analyses**.

## Agent Directives
- **MANDATORY:** Ensure inclusion/exclusion criteria are strictly defined *before* any study selection begins.
- **MANDATORY:** Include studies irrespective of publication status or language unless exclusion is explicitly and logically justified.
- **MANDATORY:** Remind the user to: "PLAN, PLAN, AND DOCUMENT!"
