# Part I: Starting a New Project (Steps 1–6)

### 1. Click "Create a new PROSPERO record..."
Log into your PROSPERO account on the NIHR platform and click the **"Create a new PROSPERO record..."** button on your dashboard.

### 2. Terms of Use
Read and understand the terms of use for registering systematic reviews, and check the confirmation box:
- `☑ I have read and understand the terms above and agree to abide by them.`

### 3. Human or Animal Population?
Choose the correct option according to your project population:
- `🔘 A systematic review of studies with people/humans as participants`
- > [!WARNING]
  > **Suspension Notice (April 2025):** The registration of animal pre-clinical reviews on PROSPERO is temporarily suspended. Systematic reviews using animal models cannot currently be registered on the platform.

### 4. Training Course Status (CRITICAL)
- **Question:** *"Is this registration record being created by a student as part of a course or module?"*
- **Mandatory Selection:**
  - `🔘 This record is NOT being created as part of a student project or training course`
- > [!CAUTION]
  > **Fatal Trap:** If you answer that this record is part of a training course, **your record will NOT be published on the public PROSPERO website**; it will only be archived internally for educational training and will not receive a valid, citable public registration ID (`CRD420...`). Even as a PhD or CTM fellow, your work is aimed at peer-reviewed publication, so you must select **`"NOT being created as part of a student project or training course"`**.

### 5. Choose Your Review Type
- **Mandatory Selection (April 2025):**
  - `🔘 Intervention review`
- > [!NOTE]
  > Currently only `"Intervention review"` is active on the NIHR PROSPERO interface; diagnostic, prognostic, and prevalence review options are labeled as "Coming soon". CTM mandates selecting `"Intervention review"` for all review designs until specialized forms become available.

### 6. Register Your Review Now
Click **"Register your review now"** to open the 12-section protocol editor.

# Part II: Content of the Protocol (Sections 1–12)

---

## Section 1: Review Title and Basic Details

### a) Review Title
- **Working title** as approved at the CTM Group Meeting / SMS defense.
- **Mandatory Suffix:** Must explicitly include `"Systematic review and Meta-analysis"`.
- *Example Format:* `"Investigating the effect of [Intervention/Exposure] on [Outcome] in [Population]: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis"`

### b) Condition or Domain Being Studied
- Select relevant PICO tags from the suggestion list.
- **Text Box:** Provide a concise background (5–6 sentences) describing the disease, intervention, or clinical condition focusing on epidemiology, clinical burden, and target biology (derived from your Group Meeting presentation).

### c) Rationale for the Review
- Briefly explain *why* this systematic review is needed and what it adds to existing scientific knowledge.
- Detail the knowledge gap (white spot), current clinical dilemmas, or unresolved conflicting evidence.

### d) Review Objectives
- State your primary research / clinical question clearly (e.g. using the PICO framework: Population, Intervention, Comparator, Outcome).

### e) Keywords
- Enter $\ge 3$ keywords strongly related to your topic separated by commas or semicolons.

### f) Country
- Select: `Hungary`.

---

## Section 2: Eligibility Criteria

### a) Population
- **Inclusion Criteria:** Clearly define the eligible clinical population (e.g., human adults $\ge 18$ years, preterm neonates $<37$ weeks GA, specific disease staging or diagnostic criteria).
- **Exclusion Criteria:** Clearly specify excluded groups (e.g., animal models, pediatric patients if adult review, severe co-morbidities, congenital malformations).

### b) Intervention(s) or Exposure(s)
- Select PICO tags corresponding to the exposure/intervention.
- **Inclusion Criteria:** Explicitly name and describe the intervention(s) or exposure(s). If analyzing comorbidities or biomarker thresholds, specify all relevant cut-offs or clinical conditions.
- **Exclusion Criteria:** State any interventions, combinations, or exposure levels that lead to study exclusion.

### c) Comparator(s) or Control(s)
- **Question:** *"Does this review compare the intervention with another treatment or with a control?"*
- Select one of the following based on study design:
  - `🔘 This review compares intervention(s) with a control or other intervention(s)` *(Standard comparative reviews)*
  - `🔘 This review includes both comparative and non-comparative studies`
  - `🔘 This review does not have any comparator(s) or control(s)` *(Single-arm prevalence/incidence reviews)*
- Assign comparator PICO tags and define inclusion/exclusion criteria for comparators (e.g., placebo, standard care, active comparator).

### d) Study Design
- **Question:** *"What kind of studies will be included?"*
  - Select: `☑ Randomised studies`, `☑ Non-randomised studies`, or both.
- **Inclusion Text Box:** Specify eligible designs (e.g., randomized controlled trials [RCTs], prospective cohorts, retrospective cohort studies).
- **Exclusion Text Box:** Explicitly exclude non-primary literature:
  > *"Case reports, case series (n < 10), reviews, editorials, letters, commentaries, and conference abstracts without full methodology."*

### e) Context
- Provide setting-specific constraints without repeating previous PICO text.
- *Examples:* Inpatient hospital / intensive care units; outpatient primary care; studies in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs); emergency department settings.

## Section 3: Similar Reviews (Pre-Search & Conflict Audit)

### a) Check for Similar Records Already in PROSPERO
- Click **"Show similar records"** to generate an automated list of existing PROSPERO registrations based on your title and criteria.
- **Audit Process:**
  1. Open and inspect each retrieved record.
  2. Compare the PICO elements, population age, comparator, and endpoints.
  3. If records are sufficiently different, document the distinction.
  4. If a substantially identical review exists:
     - > [!IMPORTANT]
       > **Mandatory Consultation:** If a very similar registration exists, immediately consult your Senior Medical Scientist (SMS) and supervisors before proceeding. You must provide a formal rationale for proceeding (e.g., outdated search date $>3$ years, differing primary outcome, broader population).

---

## Section 4: Timeline of the Review

- **Start Date:** The beginning of the academic year or project start (e.g., `2025/09/01`).
- **Anticipated Completion Date:** Exactly one year after the start date (e.g., `2026/08/31`).

## Section 6: Searching and Screening

### a) Search for Unpublished Studies
- Select: `🔘 We will search for published studies` *(unless searching trial registries like ClinicalTrials.gov for unpublished data)*.

### b) Main Bibliographic Databases
- Mandatory CTM databases (must search all three):
  - `☑ PubMed`
  - `☑ Embase`
  - `☑ Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL)`
- Additional databases if appropriate (e.g., Web of Science, Scopus). Click **"Other"** to enter additional sources.

### c) Search Language Restrictions
- Select: `🔘 No search language restrictions`
- > [!NOTE]
  > To prevent language bias, searches are run without language filters. Non-English articles may be translated during screening.

### d) Search Date Restrictions
- Usually select: `🔘 There are no search date restrictions`.
- If clinically justified (e.g., COVID-19 after Dec 2019, introduction of a novel surgical technique), select `🔘 There are search date restrictions` and state the start date and rationale.

### e) Other Methods of Identifying Studies
- Check:
  - `☑ Reference lists of eligible studies (backward citation chasing)`
  - `☑ Citation searches of included articles (forward citation chasing / CitationChaser)`
  - `☑ Searching trial registries` (if applicable)

### f) Link to Search Strategy
- Select: `🔘 Upload a search strategy file` or provide an open-access URL.
- Upload your exported search strategy document (formatted according to CTM Search Key Guide v2.6).

### g) Selection Process
- Describe the dual independent screening protocol:
  > *"Two independent reviewers will screen titles and abstracts against inclusion/exclusion criteria. Full texts of potentially eligible articles will be assessed independently by the two reviewers. Any disagreements will be resolved by consensus or arbitration by a third senior reviewer. Inter-rater reliability will be evaluated using Cohen's Kappa ($\kappa$)."*

---

## Section 7: Data Extraction, Risk of Bias & Quality Assessment

### a) Data Extraction
1. **How many people will extract data?**
   - Select: `🔘 Studies will be extracted independently by at least two people (or person/machine combination) with a process to resolve differences`.
2. **Obtaining study datasets / Individual Participant Data (IPD):**
   - Select: `🔘 No – IPD will not be sought` *(unless an IPD meta-analysis is explicitly planned)*.

### b) Study Risk of Bias or Quality Assessment
1. **Will risk of bias be assessed?**
   - Select: `🔘 Risk of bias/study quality will be assessed`.
2. **Tools to evaluate risk of bias:**
   - Select validated tools matching your included study designs:
     - **RCTs:** `RoB 2` (Cochrane Risk of Bias 2)
     - **Non-randomized intervention studies:** `ROBINS-I`
     - **Prognostic / Observational cohorts:** `QUIPS` (Quality in Prognosis Studies)
     - **Diagnostic test accuracy:** `QUADAS-2`
     - **Prevalence studies:** `JBI Critical Appraisal Tools`
3. **How many people will assess risk of bias?**
   - Select: `🔘 Studies will be assessed independently by at least two people (or person/machine combination) with a process to resolve differences`.
4. **Will additional information be sought from authors?**
   - Select: `🔘 No, additional information will not be sought from study investigators`.

### c) Reporting Bias Assessment
- Select: `🔘 No - risk of bias due to missing results will not be assessed` *(unless funnel plots/Egger tests are planned for $\ge 10$ studies)*.

### d) Certainty Assessment
- **Will certainty of evidence be assessed?**
  - Select: `🔘 Yes`.
- **Method:**
  > *"The certainty of evidence will be assessed using the GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation) approach across the domains of risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. Summary of Findings (SoF) tables will be generated."*

## Section 8: Outcomes to be Analyzed

### a) Main Outcomes (Primary Endpoints)
- Must prespecify at least **one efficacy** and **one safety** outcome.
- For each outcome, detail:
  - Exact clinical definition and diagnostic criteria.
  - Measurement instruments and assessment timepoint(s) (e.g., at 30 days, 1 year, hospital discharge).
  - Effect measure (e.g., Odds Ratio [OR], Risk Ratio [RR], Hazard Ratio [HR], Mean Difference [MD], Standardized Mean Difference [SMD]).

### b) Additional Outcomes (Secondary Endpoints)
- List all secondary clinical endpoints with matching detail.
- **Mandatory Poolability Clause:**
  > *"Secondary outcomes will be analyzed if at least three primary publications reporting on them can be identified."*

---

## Section 9: Planned Data Synthesis

### a) Strategy for Data Synthesis
1. **Is formal data synthesis planned?**
   - Select: `🔘 Formal synthesis is planned`.
2. **How will data be combined?**
   - Detail your statistical pooling strategy:
     - Model: Random-effects model (DerSimonian-Laird or restricted maximum likelihood [REML]).
     - Software: R (`metafor`, `meta` packages) or Stata.
     - Heterogeneity assessment: Cochran's $Q$ test and Higgins & Thompson $I^2$ statistic.
     - Dependent effect sizes / correlated subgroups: Multilevel Meta-Analysis with Robust Variance Estimation (RVE) with small-sample correction ($CR2$).
     - Sensitivity and subgroup analyses: Pre-specified by risk of bias, study design, and clinical severity.
